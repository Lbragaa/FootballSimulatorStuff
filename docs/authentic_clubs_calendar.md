# Real clubs and balanced venues

The opt-in development proposal is `authentic-clubs-development-1`, for 2029 onward. It uses the existing signed season-operation journal and does not add a schema or edit an accepted save. The venue policy `balanced-venues-2` also has an independent TEST precommit, so scheduling can be reviewed separately from the identity and economics decisions.

## Why 2028 has placeholder clubs

The installed production worker reports 0.13.10. Its original 2028 setup is in `braga_era/multiseason/lifecycle.py`. `setup_2028` explicitly creates generic access, Série B, regional, state and foreign entrants where it lacked prior results or qualification evidence. It sends those newly registered keys into the national qualification pathway and cup pools. The earlier 2027 registry from `braga_era/world/rosters.py` remains in the world, with recognizable names and stable IDs. It was bypassed by the 2028 entrant selection. `setup_following` would then perpetuate the placeholders through promotion, previous cup placements and foreign strength ranking. The old setup and committed outcomes remain reproducible.

## Registry, eligibility and strengths

`multiseason/club_catalogue.py` proposes 276 real clubs across the ten associations, including 163 Brazilian clubs and all 27 state federations. It documents state, country and explicit aliases. The installer reuses matched historical keys, existing strength and roster. New keys derive from a versioned country/state/name identity. Alias ambiguity or a conflicting recorded nationality stops installation. Missing country is supplied for forward selection through the catalogue without replacing historical source rows. The club lists use actual identities. Slots, registration, squad ratings and participation are fictional forward proposals. - **Série A, Série B and access.** Apply the recorded standings and existing four-up/four-down rules first. A genuine club retains its earned destination. Each legacy placeholder that would occupy one of the 20/20/8 places leaves a vacancy. Fill it from unused genuine Brazilian clubs ordered by strength and stable ID. Every replacement admission records the vacated identity and original sporting route. This is an administrative transition proposal. It assigns no result or former identity to the replacement. When all recorded participants belong to the catalogue, subsequent seasons keep normal sporting movement.
- **Copa do Brasil and Carioca.** Preserve eligible real entrants by recorded prior cup or state order. The cup retains 20 direct Série A entries and fills the remaining 108 places from previous real cup entrants and proposed federation admissions. The admissions give unrepresented states priority before filling the balance from the modeled national ranking. Carioca fills vacancies from the Rio registry. Missing historical state results remain unknown.
- **Libertadores and Sudamericana.** Brazil uses its prior real-club league and cup routes. Other associations select from a committed national model using inherited or curated strength, a small capped recent continental-title signal and a seeded annual shock of ±10. The same career and season reproduce the same order, and a club's fame grants no guaranteed place. Standard slots, overlap checks, preliminaries and draw constraints continue through the existing pipeline. A recorded defending champion can retain its special berth even if its legacy identity is fictional. This also applies where an existing domestic or continental supercup rule gives an older champion a place. It is an explicit exception to regular real-club selection.
- **Squad and financial assumptions.** Foreign starting priors are ordered by proposed competitive tier and association. Brazilian reserves span regional to promotion-level quality. New squads keep the original varied role distribution, attribute generator and stable player IDs. The model keeps inherited club power as a prior and lets future player ability and qualifying outcomes affect competition. Dynamic long-term club-power updates remain outside this version. New clubs receive a one-time hypothetical TEST opening reserve of twice the projected monthly wage base, because the shared offseason rejects clubs with less than two months of wage coverage. The exception is limited to the exact first intake year with no existing account. Existing clubs receive no opening top-up. Production financing needs explicit review.

## Player names and history

`world/player_names.py` generates one or two given names and surnames, with local and shared language pools. About 88% of draws use the host nationality and the rest use another South American pool. This is a modeled diversity parameter. Identity and nationality are stored separately from display text. The deterministic generator prevents duplicate names within each generated squad or youth intake. New youth use the same policy. Transfers preserve player IDs and name provenance.

Existing modeled players with a generic label on a reused genuine club get a separate 2029+ display alias keyed by player ID. The alias follows the player through transfers and feeds future match inputs and offseason narration. Their historical snapshots, stored names, results and numeric identities stay intact. Existing recorded players receive no alias. Older roster views that read the original database name directly can still show it.

## Fixture sequencing

The old circle used parity of both round and pairing index. For rotating clubs those changes could cancel, causing 19 straight home or away designations. The new scheme alternates the pivot by round and reverses the non-pivot pairs by pairing parity. Across tested sizes 4, 8, 20 and 32 it retains every ordered pair and limits runs to three. The national scheduler applies it only when a future-season precommit exists, before the first national fixture. Rest, venue and international-window checks continue through the existing calendar. Continental group draws and historic schedules use their existing policies.
